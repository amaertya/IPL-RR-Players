How pressure is defined

1. Win-probability model

Gradient-boosted model per innings using balls left, wickets, runs, required rate, target and format. Out-of-fold. Chase AUC 0.87.

2. Leverage = pressure

Expected swing in win probability on each ball across all outcomes. Top 20% of an innings = high pressure; bottom 60% = ordinary.

3. Value over context

Each delivery vs an average player in the same innings, over, wickets and format; a wicket is worth 10 runs. Compared with the player’s own ordinary level, shrunk for sample size.

Reliability and limitations

  • Per-ball outcomes are noisy (split-half r ≈ 0.1), so ratings are shrunk and the order within the top 10 is less certain than membership.
  • “Top-10 %” is how often a player stays in the top 10 across 54 settings (pressure threshold, wicket cost, weights, all-innings vs chase-only).
  • First-innings win probability is weaker (AUC 0.67); no opposition or role adjustment. Kohli and Rizwan were essentially tied for 10th.
