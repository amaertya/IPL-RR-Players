Methodology & Architecture

Tap a step to expand.

1 · Win-probability model

One gradient-boosted classifier per innings (balls left, wickets, runs, required rate, target, format), fitted out-of-fold by match. Chase AUC 0.87; first innings 0.67.

2 · Pressure = leverage

Leverage is the expected swing in win probability on a ball across all outcomes. The top 20% of balls in each innings (while the match is still live) are high pressure; the bottom 60% are ordinary. This captures required rate, wickets in hand, balls left and closeness together, instead of fixed cut-offs.

3 · Value over context

Every ball is scored against the average player in the same innings, over, wickets-lost bucket and format, with a wicket worth 10 runs. Batting and bowling use the same logic.

4 · Fairness & sample size

At least 90 pressure balls and 12 pressure matches; empirical-Bayes shrinkage so a few lucky balls cannot create a top-10 place; IPL and T20I baselines kept separate.

5 · Consensus ranking

Ratings are recomputed under 54 settings (threshold, wicket cost, weights, chase-only). Players earn Borda points for each top-10 finish; the consensus score is their average out of 10.

6 · Limits

Pressure skill is noisy (split-half r about 0.1), so order within the top 10 is less certain than membership. No opposition or batting-role adjustment.

Rating formula

Rating = 0.5·z(PressureValue_shrunk) + 0.2·z(WPA_shrunk) + 0.3·z(Consistency_shrunk) Value/ball (bat) = (runs − expected) − 10·(dismissed − expected)

Reliability checks

  • Win-probability model is out-of-fold, grouped by match (chase AUC 0.87, Brier 0.151).
  • Eligibility: at least 90 pressure balls and 12 pressure matches (130 batters, 136 bowlers qualify).
  • Rank stability: the final order is a Borda consensus over 54 model settings.
  • Kohli and Rizwan were statistically tied for 10th among batters.
